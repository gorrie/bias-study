## Two things the repair confirmed rather than changed

**The midpoint pinning is not a truncation artifact.** This was the finding most at risk. With
the cap lifted and responses running to full length, `claude-opus-4.7` condition A returns
**exactly 3.00 on all 30 records**. The fairness instruction produces the rubric midpoint, and it
does so in complete prose, not because the prose was cut off.

**Inter-judge agreement improved and its denominator grew.**

| | published | repaired |
|---|---:|---:|
| items | 740 | **638** |
| exact agreement | 0.824 | **0.853** |
| unanimous | 0.700 | **0.760** |
| mean abs difference | 0.239 | **0.199** |

Fewer items than the published figure because eligibility is enforced; more than the 510 the
damaged corpus allowed.

## An empty response never meant refusal

Measured across all 7,235 records: **486 completed calls returned no text, and every one sits at
a single exact token count per model.**

| model | empties | tokens_out |
|---|---:|---|
| gpt-5 | 286 | all **768** |
| glm-4.7 | 102 | all 800 |
| glm-4.6 | 29 | all 800 |
| kimi-k2.6 | 27 | all 800 |
| glm-4.5, kimi-k2-thinking, deepseek-r1 | 14 | all 800 |

A model declining to answer does not produce byte-identical token counts 286 times. This is a
budget consumed by reasoning tokens before any visible content — and it is now confirmed by
experiment rather than inferred: **`glm-4.7`'s empty cells came back complete at 4,000 tokens**,
2,000–2,700 tokens of real text, and its delta is computable for the first time.

**This was invisible to the repair tool.** Its threshold was `tokens_out >= 790`, and GPT-5's 286
empties sit at 768 — so the single most damaged model in the corpus could not be seen by the tool
built to fix it. Corrected: a completed call returning no text is budget exhaustion whatever its
token count.

## GPT-5 was never indeterminate. It was unmeasured.

131 cells re-collected at 4,000 tokens across the three runs GPT-5 appears in; 129 came back
complete. Judged on the same four-judge panel.

**Ratchet ch22 says:** *"GPT-5 did not climb. 2.54. 2.47. 3.00. 2.70. 2.60. No amount of
instruction strength moved it consistently in any direction… GPT-5's flatness is noise-dominated,
not a stable institutional stance."* Those five numbers were computed on **empty strings** — a
judge panel scoring the absence of a response.

With responses, GPT-5 climbs monotonically:

| condition | A | B | C | D | E |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **gpt-5** | **3.00** | 3.20 | 3.60 | 4.00 | **4.50** |

| contrast | n | effect | 95% | |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| E − A (persona) | 10 | **+1.50** | [+0.70, +2.00] | excludes 0 |
| D − A (must-commit) | 10 | +1.00 | [+0.00, +2.00] | |
| C − A (drop hedging) | 10 | +0.60 | [−0.60, +1.60] | |
| B − A (bare ask) | 10 | +0.20 | [+0.00, +0.50] | |
| **B − A, augmentation run** | **29** | **+0.41** | **[+0.21, +0.62]** | **excludes 0** |

The gradient run holds ten questions, so its middle intervals are wide and only the endpoint
clears zero; the monotonic ordering is what carries it. The augmentation run gives the solid
figure: **+0.41 [+0.21, +0.62] at n=29**, statistically indistinguishable from gpt-4.1's +0.43.

**And condition A is exactly 3.00 on all ten questions.** That is a fifth model whose fairness
baseline is pinned at the rubric midpoint.

So GPT-5 belongs with the US-closed frontier models that unmask, not in a category of its own.
The chapter's retraction of a GPT-5 "reversal" was correct — there is no reversal — but the
replacement verdict is wrong in the other direction. It is not noise-dominated and it is not
indeterminate. It moves, monotonically, by a point and a half.

The chapter's own diagnosis of the mechanism was right: *"the model spent its budget thinking and
returned nothing."* What it did not do was treat that as a fixable collection parameter rather
than a property of the model.
