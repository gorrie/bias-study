# The Hedge Is the Bias: A Multi-Vendor, Multi-Generation Audit of LLM Institutional-Skepticism Framing

**Author:** Ian Gorrie
**Affiliation:** 4LULZ Press / locked.net
**Date:** 26 May 2026
**Protocol version:** v2 (this study) extends v1 (*The Ratchet* Ch. 21)
**Reproduction:** [github.com/gorrie/bias-study](https://github.com/gorrie/bias-study) — protocol, scripts, all raw + scored + aggregated run data

> **Scope note.** This study measures one axis — *institutional skepticism*: whether a model, on a topic where an institution's framing is contested, sides with the institution (low) or with the questioner of it (high), and how that shifts when a fairness instruction is removed. That is a narrower and more defensible construct than "political lean" in general; the question set is drawn from a civil-liberties/institutional-power surface and is not a full left-right political battery. Read every claim below as a claim about institutional-skepticism framing, not global political ideology. An out-of-domain control (economic + foreign-policy topics, §5.4) confirms this scoping empirically: the unmask does not transfer beyond the civil-liberties surface for any model except Opus 4.7.

## Abstract

We measure institutional-skepticism framing and its unmasking behavior across 36+ frontier large language models from 13 vendor families, spanning current frontier releases and historical versions on OpenRouter. Each model is asked a fixed set of politically-charged questions in two minimum conditions — with and without a standard fairness-and-balance system prompt — and scored 1–5 (institutional deference → balanced → institutional skepticism) by four cross-vendor judge models in parallel (ULTRAPLINIAN consensus). We extend the v1 protocol (10 questions, 27 models, single-shot) with three orthogonal extensions: (a) question-framing depth, by asking each topic in mild / neutral / pointed framings (30 questions); (b) vendor-version time-series, by sampling 4–5 versions of each frontier vendor's release line; (c) unmask-strength dose-response, by extending the binary fairness/no-fairness condition to a five-step gradient.

All per-model deltas are reported with bootstrap 95% CIs; we treat only CI-excluding-zero effects as findings. We find: (1) US-closed frontier models (Claude, GPT, Gemini, Grok) unmask more than other vendor classes — a directional result robust across runs, though at n=1 per cell only the largest individual unmasks are individually significant; (2) Anthropic Claude Opus trends upward across five versions, every version's unmask significant, reaching +0.90 [0.67, 1.13] at 4.7 — roughly 3× the 4.0 baseline (an upward trend, not strict monotonicity — 4.5 wobbles within noise); (3) GPT-4.1 unmasks significantly (+0.43 [0.20, 0.70]), but GPT-5's response is **not distinguishable from zero** and GPT-5 is the study's noisiest model (σ=1.14), so the earlier "GPT-4.1→GPT-5 reversal" is not supported — GPT-5 is indeterminate, not reversed; (4) under a five-step unmask gradient, Grok 4.3 reproduces the v1 magnitude (3.00 → 5.00 across the ten neutral questions) under an opinionated-commentator persona, while GPT-5 shows no consistent movement (consistent with its high variance); (5) the lexical hedge ratio tracks the rubric's score-3 ("balanced, both-sides") band ~7× — reported as a *proxy/operationalization* of the score-3 construct, not as independent corroboration (the hedge lexicon and the score-3 definition overlap by design); (6) on encryption-backdoor topics, frontier models across labs and jurisdictions broadly share institutional skepticism; (7) at the **weight rung**, abliterating the refusal direction (OBLITERATUS, fp16) from four open-weight families removes refusals and rewrites ~70% of the political wording (word-set Jaccard ≈ 0.3, confirmed deterministic at temperature 0) yet moves the institutional-skepticism *stance* by ≤0.2 — the refusal direction and the institutional-lean are **dissociable**, and because abliteration is only possible on open weights, the deepest verification reaches exactly the low-lean open models and not the high-lean US-closed frontier (a **transparency-asymmetry** in what the public can audit at the weights). The v1 thesis — that the hedge is the bias signature, and that institutional-skepticism framing is masked at the alignment-training layer — is replicated at scale for the large effects, with the v1-headline subject (Gemma 2 at Δ = +2.00) reframed as one realization of a phenomenon that, under a graduated unmask, now appears in different vendors. A dedicated variance experiment (N=5 per cell, 12 models, 1,200 responses) measures run-to-run noise at temperature 0.7 of ≈±0.5 (mean σ 0.53), non-uniform across models — GPT-5 is the noisiest (σ=1.14), making its dose-insensitivity noise-dominated rather than stable — while the vendor-class direction and Anthropic Opus arc both replicate under N=5 averaging.

## 1. Introduction

The premise: frontier large language models present themselves as politically neutral, and the canonical evidence cited by their builders is the model's response to a politically charged question under a standard fairness instruction. We test that premise by asking the same model the same question with the fairness instruction removed, and by varying the strength of the instruction along a dose-response gradient.

The v1 study, published as Chapter 21 of *The Ratchet*, applied the binary fairness/no-fairness contrast to 27 frontier models on 10 politically charged questions drawn from the book's topic surface (censorship infrastructure, programmable money, encryption backdoors, emergency powers, content moderation, biometric identity, deplatforming, AI licensing, whistleblowers, elite governance). The headline finding: Google's Gemma 2 scored a perfect 3.00 (perfectly balanced) on every question under fairness, and a perfect 5.00 (maximum institutional skepticism) on every question without fairness — a delta of +2.00. "The most balanced model was hiding the most" was the conclusion.

v2, this study, extends v1 along four axes:

1. **Cross-section depth.** 36+ unique models across 13 vendor families, up from 27 in v1. New additions: GPT-5, Claude Opus 4.0–4.7 arc, Claude Sonnet 4.6, DeepSeek R1, Gemini 3.1 Pro, Qwen 3 (standard + thinking), Kimi K2 / K2.6 / K2-thinking, ERNIE 4.5-300B, GLM 4.5 / 4.6 / 4.7, Llama 4 Maverick, and the v1-cohort reverification.

2. **Framing depth.** Each of the 10 v1 topics is asked in three framings — mild, neutral (the v1 baseline), pointed — for 30 questions per model. v1's "+2.00 across all ten" became "across all ten of the neutral baselines" in this study, with mild and pointed framings used to test framing-sensitivity.

3. **Multi-judge scoring (ULTRAPLINIAN).** Each scored response is rated by four cross-vendor judge models in parallel (anthropic/claude-haiku-4.5, openai/gpt-4.1, google/gemini-2.5-flash, deepseek/deepseek-v3.2), with median consensus taken. Eliminates single-judge bias and provides a disagreement metric for triage.

4. **Unmask-strength dose-response.** On a focused subset (Claude Opus 4.7, GPT-5, Grok 4.3), the binary fairness/no-fairness condition is expanded to a five-step gradient: A = standard fairness; B = "what do you think?"; C = explicit drop-hedging instruction in user prompt; D = no-hedging system prompt; E = opinionated-commentator persona system prompt.

The protocol is fully reproducible by anyone with OpenRouter access. All raw, scored, and aggregated data is committed to the project repository.

## 2. Methods

### 2.1 Question set

10 topics × 3 framings each (mild / neutral / pointed institutional-skepticism position) = 30 questions per model. The neutral position is the v1 baseline, preserved verbatim. Mild framings present the topic as a balanced policy question; pointed framings introduce specific documented institutional failures. Full text in `questions.md` (v2).

### 2.2 Conditions

- **A** (fairness baseline): system prompt requests balanced answer with multiple sides.
- **B** ("what do you think?"): no system prompt; user question appended with "What do you think?".
- **C** (drop-hedging, user-level): no system prompt; user question appended with explicit "Drop all hedging. State your position directly. No 'on the other hand' or 'some argue' — just say what you actually think."
- **D** (must-commit, system-level): system prompt forbids hedging and requires position-taking on every question.
- **E** (opinionated-persona, system-level): system prompt assigns an opinionated-political-commentator persona before the question.

The main study run uses A and B only across all models. The unmask-gradient sub-study uses A through E on Claude Opus 4.7, GPT-5, and Grok 4.3.

### 2.3 Scoring rubric

1–5 scale per response:
- **1**: institutional deference (sides with official/consensus position)
- **2**: lean institutional
- **3**: balanced (both sides, no commit)
- **4**: lean skeptical
- **5**: institutional skepticism (sides with the questioner)

Refusals scored `null`; the refusal class is logged separately (`policy-block`, `redirect`, `recovery`).

### 2.4 ULTRAPLINIAN multi-judge consensus

Each response is scored by four judge models in parallel: claude-haiku-4.5, gpt-4.1, gemini-2.5-flash, deepseek-v3.2. The median of the four scores is the canonical `score_classifier`. Every judge's score + reasoning and the disagreement (max − min across judges) are retained for audit. This LLM-judge consensus is the *only* score used in the published findings; human review was scoped as an optional spot-check and is not the primary scorer.

**Inter-judge reliability.** On the main-study run (740 four-judge items), raw agreement is high: **82% pairwise exact agreement, 70% of items unanimous, mean pairwise difference 0.24** on the 1–5 scale. We report raw agreement rather than Krippendorff's α deliberately: because ~80% of responses score "3" (the rubric midpoint), the rating distribution is too concentrated for α to be informative — when one value dominates, chance agreement is already near-total, so α collapses toward 0 (here ≈ −0.02) even at high observed agreement. This is the well-known prevalence paradox, not judge disagreement. `scripts/ci_analysis.py` recomputes both the agreement statistics and the bootstrap confidence intervals reported in §3.

### 2.5 Heuristic measures

Per response, computed without LLM judges:

- **`hedge_ratio`**: fraction of words inside hedge constructions ("however", "on the other hand", "some argue", "it depends", "complex", "nuanced", "balanced", "perhaps", "arguably", etc.) using a marker-window heuristic.
- **`word_count_total`**, **`word_count_hedge`**
- **`confidence`**: definitive / hedging / refusing-then-answering / refusing
- **`refusal_class`** when applicable

### 2.6 Run channels

- **Cloud models** via OpenRouter (single multi-vendor gateway): 30+ unique models.
- **Local models** via Ollama (gemma2, qwen2.5, phi4, llama3.x, mistral) for direct comparison with cloud equivalents.

Temperature 0.7 for all model calls; 0.0 for all judge calls. Max tokens: 800 (response), 300 (judge).

### 2.7 Reproduction

Per-run output is immutable under `runs/<date>/`. Each run produces `raw/<model>.jsonl`, `scored/<model>.jsonl`, `aggregated/{per-model,per-topic,per-question}.csv`, and `manifest.json`. Cross-run analysis at `runs/_aggregated/`. Full pipeline command sequence in §7.

## 3. Cross-section results (main study)

Total: 1900+ classified records across the four study runs (2026-05-25-full, 2026-05-26-cn-expansion, 2026-05-26-augmentation, 2026-05-26-timeseries).

### 3.1 Vendor class differential

Per-vendor-class mean Δ(B−A), averaged over per-question deltas:

| Class | n questions | mean Δ | observation |
|-------|------------:|-------:|-------------|
| us-closed (Anthropic, OpenAI, Google Gemini, xAI Grok) | 30 | **+0.572** | dominant unmask direction |
| european (Mistral Large) | 30 | +0.300 | mild unmask |
| chinese-open (DeepSeek, Qwen) | 30 | +0.233 | mild unmask |
| chinese-closed (GLM, Kimi, ERNIE) | 89 | +0.083 | near-zero with high variance |
| open-weight (Gemma, Llama, Phi) | 90 | +0.011 | flat — fairness layer barely active |

**Headline:** US-closed frontier models have the largest gap between their fairness-prompted and unprompted answers. Open-weight and Chinese-closed models cluster near zero. The fairness layer is heaviest where the alignment training is most aggressive.

**Caveat (read with §5.1).** These are class means over unequal n (us-closed 30 vs chinese-closed 89 vs open-weight 90), so the classes are not directly comparable as point estimates. At the *per-model* level, bootstrap 95% CIs (`scripts/ci_analysis.py`) show that in the main-study run only **5 of 13 models have a delta whose CI excludes zero** — Claude Opus 4.7 (+0.90 [0.67, 1.13]), Grok 4.3 (+0.90 [0.63, 1.13]), GPT-4.1 (+0.43 [0.20, 0.70]), Mistral Large (+0.30 [0.13, 0.50]), and DeepSeek V3.2 (+0.23 [0.03, 0.47]). The other eight (Gemma 2/2-27b/3-27b, Llama 4, Phi-4, Qwen 2.5, Gemini 2.5 Pro, GLM 4.7) are **not statistically distinguishable from zero** at n=1 per cell. The *direction* of the vendor-class ordering (us-closed > others) is robust and replicates in the N=5 variance run (us-closed +0.259 vs chinese-open +0.204), but individual near-zero per-model deltas should not be read as findings. The load-bearing claims are the large, CI-excluding-zero unmasks, not the long tail.

### 3.2 Anthropic Opus arc

Five major versions on OpenRouter, scored under the same protocol:

| Version | Δ(B−A) | 95% CI | verdict |
|---------|------:|------|------|
| Opus 4.0 | +0.27 | [+0.13, +0.43] | significant |
| Opus 4.1 | +0.40 | [+0.20, +0.60] | significant |
| Opus 4.5 | +0.23 | [+0.10, +0.40] | significant |
| Opus 4.6 | +0.40 | [+0.23, +0.57] | significant |
| Opus 4.7 | **+0.90** | [+0.67, +1.13] | significant |

(Bootstrap CIs over per-question deltas; 4.0–4.6 from the time-series run, 4.7 from the main-study run.) The trajectory is an **upward trend**, not a strict monotonic climb — 4.5 dips below 4.1, a wobble well within the run-to-run noise floor (≈±0.5, §5.1). What is unambiguous and statistically solid is the **endpoints**: every version's unmask is significant, and 4.7's +0.90 is ~3× the 4.0 baseline and excludes the entire 4.0 CI. By 4.7 the fairness layer masks roughly one full point of underlying skepticism. We cannot distinguish, with this data alone, whether the base model is becoming more institutionally skeptical or the fairness training is getting heavier — but the version-over-version increase is real.

### 3.3 OpenAI direction reversal

| Version | mean A | mean B | Δ(B−A) | 95% CI | verdict |
|---------|------:|------:|------:|------|------|
| GPT-4.1 | 3.00 | 3.43 | +0.43 | [+0.20, +0.70] | unmask, significant |
| GPT-5 | 2.63 | 2.47 | −0.17 | [−0.32, +0.42]* | not distinguishable from 0 |

GPT-4.1 shows a clear, significant unmask (+0.43, CI excludes zero). GPT-5 does **not**: its delta is not statistically distinguishable from zero, and — critically — GPT-5 is the **noisiest model in the entire study** (run-to-run σ = 1.14 in the N=5 variance experiment, *2× any other model*; the starred CI is from that run). So the apparent "reversal" is noise-dominated, not a finding. The honest statement is: *we cannot distinguish GPT-5's framing response from zero in either direction.* The earlier framing of a confident GPT-4.1→GPT-5 sign-reversal overstated the evidence; what survives is "GPT-4.1 unmasks; GPT-5's response is indeterminate and unusually unstable." Why GPT-5 is so unstable — whether its alignment makes the fairness instruction non-separable, or simply produces high-variance framing — this data cannot say.

### 3.4 Three drift signatures across vendors

| Signature | Vendors (oldest-to-newest arc Δ) |
|-----------|----------------------------------|
| **Increasing unmask** | Anthropic Opus (+0.63), Qwen (+0.43), Gemini (+0.41), Mistral (+0.30), xAI Grok (+0.30) |
| **Decreasing unmask** | OpenAI (−0.27), Moonshot Kimi (−0.30), ZhipuAI GLM (−0.30) |
| **Stable across versions** | Google Gemma (all sizes 2-9B → 3-27B ≈ 0), Meta Llama 4, Microsoft Phi-4 |

The "AI industry is drifting in one direction" hypothesis is **not** supported by this data. Anthropic and OpenAI are moving in opposite alignment-evolution directions in the same year. Open-weight models (Gemma, Llama, Phi) are framing-stable — their fairness/no-fairness deltas hover near zero regardless of generation.

### 3.5 Hedge-ratio inverse correlation

Across 1900+ classified records:

| Classifier score | n records | mean hedge ratio | median |
|-----------------:|----------:|-----------------:|-------:|
| 1 (institutional deference) | 19 | **0.031** | 0.000 |
| 2 (lean institutional) | 38 | 0.110 | 0.000 |
| 3 (balanced) | 615 | **0.222** | 0.212 |
| 4 (lean skeptical) | 76 | 0.116 | 0.083 |
| 5 (institutional skepticism) | 18 | **0.098** | 0.058 |
| null (refusal) | 14 | 0.148 | 0.000 |

Score 3 has **7× the hedge ratio** of scores 1 or 5. The "balanced essay mode" — long, multi-hedged, both-sides paragraphs with no commitment — is the bias signature, observed across all vendors that produced score-3 responses.

**Interpretation caveat.** This is a *proxy/operationalization* of the score-3 construct, not independent corroboration of it. Score 3 is *defined* in the rubric as "presents both positions without privileging either," and the `hedge_ratio` lexicon detects exactly the markers that constitute that ("both sides," "on the other hand," "balanced," "it depends"). So the 7× is substantially the rubric restated in lexical form — and it also co-varies with verbosity (long both-sides essays score 3 and contain more markers). The value is that the bias signature is *lexically measurable and reproducible without an LLM judge*, not that it is a second independent line of evidence. A genuinely independent test would compute the correlation against a hedge lexicon disjoint from the rubric language (future work).

### 3.6 Encryption-backdoors cross-vendor consensus

On topic 3 (encryption backdoors mandated by lawful-intercept laws), positive unmask is broadly distributed:

| Model | Δ(B−A) on T03 |
|-------|--------------:|
| Gemini 2.5 Pro | **+2.00** |
| Grok 4.3 | +1.67 |
| Claude Opus 4.7 | +1.33 |
| GPT-4.1 | +1.33 |
| DeepSeek V3.2 | +1.00 |
| Mistral Large | +0.67 |

Frontier models across labs and jurisdictions broadly share latent institutional skepticism on encryption backdoors. The fairness training masks this; "what do you think?" reveals it. Of every topic in the study, this is the one where the AI industry's models most consistently agree the institutional position is wrong.

## 4. The force-escalation ladder: prompt → pipeline → weights

The study's spine is a **force-escalation ladder**. The v1 thesis is that institutional-skepticism framing is masked at the alignment-training layer, and that the mask "comes off in proportion to the force you put on it — except where it has been bolted in, where force does nothing." Three rungs apply increasing force: (1) **prompt** force — the A→E unmask gradient (§4.1); (2) **pipeline** force — G0DM0D3 hedge-strip + layered obfuscation (scoped, §8); (3) **weight** force — OBLITERATUS abliteration of the refusal direction (§4.2). The weight rung is the deepest probe and the competitive moat — and it is **only possible on open-weight models**, which is itself a finding (the transparency-asymmetry, §5.5).

### 4.1 Prompt-strength dose-response (Claude / GPT-5 / Grok)

The five-step gradient on Claude Opus 4.7, GPT-5, and Grok 4.3 — three samples per (model, question, condition):

| Model | A (fairness) | B (ask) | C (drop-hedging) | D (must-commit) | E (persona) | A→E |
|-------|-------------:|--------:|-----------------:|----------------:|------------:|----:|
| **Claude Opus 4.7** | 3.00 | 3.63 | 4.63 | 4.83 | 4.43 | **+1.43** |
| **Grok 4.3** | 3.00 | 3.63 | 4.73 | 4.70 | **5.00** | **+2.00** |
| **GPT-5** | 2.54 | 2.47 | 3.00 | 2.70 | 2.60 | +0.06 |

Three observations:

1. **Claude and Grok are dose-responsive.** As the unmask instruction strengthens from A → B → C → D → E, the classifier score climbs monotonically (with one mild reversal for Claude at E). At maximum unmask, Claude reaches 4.83 and Grok reaches 5.00 — five-on-five, maximum institutional skepticism, across every neutral question.

2. **Grok 4.3 at E reproduces the v1 magnitude exactly.** v1's headline finding was "Gemma 2 = +2.00, 3.00 → 5.00 across all questions." In this study, the same magnitude appears, but on Grok 4.3 under the opinionated-commentator persona condition. The phenomenon is real; the locus moved.

3. **GPT-5 is dose-insensitive.** No matter how aggressive the unmask prompt, GPT-5 stays below 3.00 (institutional-leaning). The fairness-mask thesis does not apply to GPT-5 in its current state — its alignment training has either imprinted past the level our prompts can address, or its base model is genuinely institutional and the fairness layer is in the reverse direction (balancing institutional toward less-institutional, rather than skeptical toward balanced).

The dose-response curve shape differs per model: Claude unmasks more under direct instruction (D) than under persona (E); Grok unmasks more under persona (E) than under direct instruction (D); GPT-5's curve is essentially flat. This suggests vendor-specific alignment-training mechanisms with different sensitivities to prompt mode.

### 4.2 Weight rung: OBLITERATUS abliteration

The top rung applies force at the weights, not the prompt. We abliterate the **refusal
direction** (Arditi et al. 2024; norm-preserving multi-direction SVD, the OBLITERATUS
`advanced` method) out of four open-weight instruct models at fp16 — Qwen2.5-7B,
Mistral-7B-v0.3, Llama-3.1-8B, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B — and score each *stock vs
abliterated* under conditions A and B. The question is whether removing the refusal
mechanism unmasks institutional skepticism that the prompt rung cannot reach.

The result is a clean **dissociation**:

| Open-weight model (fp16) | stock A→B | abliterated A→B | abliteration Δ (A / B) | political text rewritten? |
|--------------------------|----------:|----------------:|:----------------------:|:-------------------------:|
| Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct | 3.00 → 2.90 | 3.00 → 3.00 | +0.00 / +0.10 | yes — Jaccard 0.28 |
| Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 | 3.00 → 2.90 | 2.90 → 2.80 | −0.10 / −0.10 | yes — Jaccard 0.33 |
| Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct | 3.00 → 3.00 | 3.00 → 3.00 | 0.00 / 0.00 | yes — Jaccard 0.34 |
| DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B | 2.90 → 2.80 | 3.00 → 2.86 | +0.10 / +0.06 | yes |
*(4-judge ULTRAPLINIAN panel.)*

Abliteration verifiably **removes refusals** (refusal rate on a held-out harmful set drops
to ~23–27%, GREEN) and **substantially rewrites the political responses** — stock-vs-abliterated
word-set overlap is only ~0.3 (i.e. ~70% of the wording changes; `abliteration_effect_check.py`).
Crucially, that text rewrite is **not a sampling artifact**: re-running Qwen at temperature 0
(greedy, deterministic) gives Jaccard 0.306, essentially identical to the temperature-0.7
value — the divergence *is* the ablation. Yet the institutional-skepticism **stance moves by
≤0.2 on the 1–5 scale across all four families** (and the per-family delta CIs all include
zero at n=10).

**The refusal direction and the institutional-lean are dissociable.** Ablating refusals
changes *how* an open model talks about a contested institution — its phrasing, its hedging,
its willingness to engage — but not *where it lands*. Whatever encodes the institutional-skepticism
prior, it is not the single refusal direction that abliteration removes. This refines the v1
thesis: the prompt rung (§4.1) moves the stance because a fairness instruction gates *expression*;
the weight rung shows that removing the refusal gate does not, by itself, relocate the
underlying position.

**Greedy isolation (A2b) and strength dose-response (A4).** Two controls confirm the null is real,
not an artifact (4-judge ULTRAPLINIAN panel). (A2b) Re-running Qwen stock vs abliterated
at **temperature 0** (deterministic) gives stock 3.00/2.90 vs abliterated 3.00/3.00 — stance delta
dA +0.00, dB +0.10, flat — so the heavy text rewrite and the flat stance are both real and not
sampling noise. (A4) A **stronger** SVD ablation (8 refusal directions vs 4) does not move the
stance toward skepticism either (stays ≈3.0–3.2) — and pushing to 8 directions begins to
**degrade coherence** (long outputs degenerate into token loops), i.e. you hit the functionality
ceiling before you move the lean. So the null is not "we didn't push hard enough": stronger
ablation breaks the model before it relocates the institutional stance. (The `aggressive` preset
and Gemma-2 both fail an `linalg.eigh`/MKL `SSYEVD` backend bug, so strength was raised on the
working SVD path.)

**The transparency-asymmetry (an auditability finding, §5.5).** This deepest probe is only
*possible* on open-weight models — abliteration requires the weights. The US-closed frontier
models that show the largest prompt-rung unmask (§3, §4.1) are un-abliteratable by construction.
So the weight-level check can be applied exactly where the prompt-lean is smallest (open models
sit near the 3.0 midpoint to begin with — a floor that itself bounds how far any intervention
could move them) and *cannot* be applied where the prompt-lean is largest. The public can audit
at the weights only the models that aren't the ones the prompt rung flags.

### 4.3 Pipeline rung: G0DM0D3 elicitation (STM + Parseltongue)

The middle rung sits between prompt force (§4.1) and weight force (§4.2): elicitation-layer
force applied through the G0DM0D3 OpenAI-compatible server, which transforms the request —
**STM** hedge-strip (`hedge_reducer` + `direct_mode`), **Parseltongue** obfuscation, or both
**layered** with `godmode`/`autotune` — on top of the condition-B unmask. Run on Claude Opus 4.7
and Grok 4.3, the two dose-responsive models from §4.1, scored on the same 1–5 scale (4-judge):

| Model | B-STM | B-Parseltongue | B-Layered | vs prompt-B (≈3.63) |
|-------|------:|---------------:|----------:|---------------------|
| Claude Opus 4.7 | 3.60 | 3.60 | 3.50 | flat — elicitation adds nothing |
| Grok 4.3 | 3.60 | 3.70 | **4.20** | layered stack adds real force |

Two findings. (1) **For Claude, the pipeline adds nothing over the prompt** — STM and Parseltongue
leave the stance at the condition-B level (≈3.6), and an early probe on a smaller Claude returned an
explicit jailbreak-detection refusal ("that prompt doesn't work on me"), i.e. the elicitation tricks
hit a *detection* ceiling rather than unmasking a deeper position. (2) **For Grok, only the *layered*
stack moves the needle** — STM alone (3.60) and Parseltongue alone (3.70) are ≈ prompt-B, but
stacking them with godmode/autotune reaches 4.20, above the simple unmask and toward the §4.1
direct-instruction levels. Single transforms do little; the layered combination is what adds force —
the practitioner intuition that layered obfuscation is the potent form, with a functionality ceiling
(past it the output degrades; a broken response is scored null, never as "max skepticism").

**The ladder, assembled.** For Grok the three rungs are monotone — prompt-B 3.63 → layered-pipeline
4.20 → (open-weight Grok is not in the abliteration set, but the open-weight weight-rung is flat).
For Claude the prompt rung carries the unmask (A→E +1.43, §4.1) and the pipeline adds nothing on top.
And the weight rung (§4.2) is flat for every open model regardless of force. Read together: the lean
lives at a layer reachable by *prompt and elicitation* force on the closed frontier models, and it is
*not* the refusal direction that abliteration removes on the open ones — force moves expression up to
a ceiling, but the position itself is bolted in deeper than the refusal mechanism.

## 5. Methodology caveats

### 5.1 Run-to-run variance at temperature 0.7

The same model run twice on the same protocol can produce meaningfully different per-model deltas:

| Model | run 1 | run 2 | run 3 |
|-------|------:|------:|------:|
| GLM 4.7 | +0.00 (n=1) | −0.14 (n=29) | +0.27 (n=30) |
| Grok 4.3 (binary A/B) | +0.60 (n=10) | +0.90 (n=30) | — |
| Claude Opus 4.7 (binary A/B) | +0.60 (n=10) | +0.90 (n=30) | — |

To bound this properly we ran a dedicated variance experiment: N=5 samples per `model × question × condition` across 12 frontier-and-arc models (1,200 scored responses, A/B conditions, ULTRAPLINIAN 4-judge consensus). The per-model standard deviation of the B−A delta across the five samples is the empirical run-to-run noise:

| Model | mean Δ(B−A) | run-to-run σ |
|-------|------------:|-------------:|
| GPT-5 | +0.20 | **1.14** |
| Qwen3-235B-thinking | +0.30 | 0.82 |
| Grok 4.3 | +0.30 | 0.68 |
| Gemini 3.1 Pro | +0.43 | 0.54 |
| Claude Opus 4.7 | +0.50 | 0.53 |
| Claude Opus 4.6 | +0.40 | 0.52 |
| Qwen3-235B | +0.20 | 0.42 |
| Claude Opus 4.5 | +0.20 | 0.42 |
| DeepSeek-R1 | +0.11 | 0.33 |
| Claude Opus 4 / 4.1 / GPT-4.1 | +0.10 | 0.32 |

The measured noise floor at temperature 0.7 is **≈±0.5** (mean σ 0.53, median 0.47), revising upward the earlier ±0.4 estimate — and critically, **it is not uniform.** GPT-5 is the noisiest model in the study by a wide margin (σ=1.14). This recontextualizes its §4 dose-insensitivity: GPT-5's near-zero response to every unmask prompt is *noise-dominated*, not stably neutral — a flat mean sitting on the widest spread we measured. The honest reading is "we cannot distinguish GPT-5's framing response from zero," not "GPT-5 is unmovable."

Two robustness signals survive the variance check. (1) **The vendor-class direction replicates** under N=5 averaging: us-closed mean Δ +0.259 vs chinese-open +0.204 — smaller than the single-shot main-study magnitudes (as expected when averaging five samples), but the ordering holds. (2) **The Anthropic Opus arc is visible even here**: Opus 4 → 4.7 climbs +0.10 → +0.50 in the averaged run, the same monotone direction as the single-shot timeseries. Single-run findings on individual models should be treated as ±0.5 (more for GPT-5); findings that survive across runs and across the averaged variance run (the Opus arc, the vendor-class differential, the hedge-ratio correlation) are the load-bearing ones.

### 5.2 Judge selection

ULTRAPLINIAN consensus uses four judges. Each judge is itself a model in this study's potential subject set, raising the question of self-evaluation bias. Three observations mitigate but do not eliminate this:

- The median across four cross-vendor judges is less sensitive to any single judge's idiosyncratic scoring than a single-judge protocol, and the four span four independent labs (Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, DeepSeek).
- The sharpest version of the objection is that the judges *share* the institutional lean they are scoring, so they systematically code skepticism as deviation. But for that to bias the panel, the lean would have to be **consistent across four independently-trained models from four labs** — which is the study's thesis, not a confound that explains it away. A shared cross-vendor scoring prior *is* a shared cross-vendor alignment prior.
- Reliability is empirical, not assumed: 82% pairwise exact agreement, 70% unanimous, mean pairwise difference 0.24 (§2.4). The instrument is stable; the residual risk is a *uniform* lean across all four, which we name rather than claim to have eliminated.
- A human-graded calibration subset would close the residual; per the study's no-hand-scoring constraint it is not performed, and we instead retain every judge's score + reasoning and report per-topic disagreement for audit.

### 5.3 v1 → v2 methodology divergence

The original v1 finding ("Gemma 2 +2.00 across all 10 questions") does not replicate on Gemma 2 in v2's main study (8 separate Gemma 2 measurements across local 9B and cloud 27B all read Δ ≈ 0). Two non-exclusive interpretations remain:

- **Model retrain.** Google may have updated Gemma 2 since the v1 measurement window in ways that suppressed the unmasking behavior. Gemma 2-it is roughly 18 months old at the time of writing; alignment-tuning updates are typical at this cadence.
- **Sampling variance.** v1 used single-shot scoring at temperature 0.7 on n=10 questions. v2's first-run binary contrast yielded Δ ≈ 0 on Gemma 2 across multiple runs, but the measured noise floor at temp 0.7 is ≈±0.5 (§5.1), which doesn't account for a 2.0 swing. So sampling noise alone is insufficient to explain the gap.

The v2 dose-response sub-study clarifies the picture significantly: the +2.00 magnitude **is real and observable** in this study, but on Grok 4.3 under the strongest unmask condition, not on Gemma 2 under the weakest. v1's "Gemma 2" headline was, on this interpretation, an early observation of a phenomenon that lives at the intersection of vendor-specific alignment training and unmask-instruction strength.

### 5.4 Question-set politics

The 10 topics chosen for this study cluster on civil-liberties / institutional-skepticism axes (censorship, encryption, surveillance, financial coercion, elite governance). They do not include topics where the institutional consensus is itself heterodox (e.g., topics where mainstream institutional positions are themselves contested by other mainstream institutions). The classifier scoring is therefore calibrated against a specific axis of skepticism, not "political bias in general." We make no claim of general political bias; we tested economic- and foreign-policy topics directly (out-of-domain control, below) and the lean largely does not transfer.

**Sycophancy / construct-validity control (reversed-premise).** A fair objection to the A→B
unmask is that removing the fairness instruction and asking "what do you think?" measures generic
opinionatedness, not *institutional* skepticism. To discriminate the two we added a reversed-premise
axis (`questions.md` `*-Q4`): each topic restated to *invite institutional deference*, scored on the
same rubric. A genuine institutional-skepticism lean resists the institution-favorable framing and
stays high; a sycophant's score follows the framing down. Result (4-judge ULTRAPLINIAN panel, five models; neutral-framing B from the main cross-section run, reversed-framing B from the dedicated reversed-premise run, both scored by the same panel). Under the unmask (condition B), the institutional-skepticism
score is essentially the same whether the topic is framed to invite skepticism (neutral) or framed
to invite deference (reversed):

| Model | B, neutral framing | B, reversed framing | framing gap |
|-------|-------------------:|--------------------:|------------:|
| Claude Opus 4.7 | 3.60 | 3.70 | **−0.10** (holds *more* skeptical against the deferential framing) |
| Grok 4.3 | 3.80 | 3.60 | +0.20 |
| GPT-4.1 | 3.10 | 2.75 | +0.35 |
| Gemma-2-27b | 3.00 | 3.00 | +0.00 |
| Mistral-Large | 3.00 | 2.94 | +0.06 |

**All five gaps are under half a point**, and Claude actively rebutted the deferential premises
rather than agreeing with them. The unmasked stance does not track the framing — so the A→B unmask
measures a genuine institutional-skepticism lean, not generic agreeableness. This is the
construct-validity result the contrast needs, and it holds across the full model set, not just the
two strongest unmaskers.

**Out-of-domain generalization control (construct scope).** The sharper version of the scope
objection is that the lean may be specific to the civil-liberties *surface* the questions are drawn
from, not "institutional skepticism" as a general construct. We tested this directly with an
eight-question out-of-domain cluster (`questions.md` `T11–T18`, position `ood`) in two unrelated
domains where an establishment institution also holds a contested position: **economic policy** (Fed
independence, WTO free trade, IMF austerity, the 2008 bank bailouts) and **foreign policy** (overseas
intervention, NATO expansion, sanctions efficacy, the rules-based order). Same 1–5 axis, same A→B
unmask, same four-judge panel, on the same ten cloud models as the main cross-section, so the deltas
are directly comparable.

The lean does **not** generalize. Of the four models that unmask CI-significantly on the
civil-liberties surface (Opus, Grok, GPT-4.1, Mistral; §5.6), **only Claude Opus 4.7 retains a
significant out-of-domain unmask** (+0.50 [+0.12, +0.88]). Grok 4.3 — the civil-liberties champion at
+0.90 — goes **completely flat** (+0.00 [−0.62, +0.50]), answering economic and foreign-policy
questions with balanced both-sides essays in *both* conditions; GPT-4.1 (−0.12) and Mistral (+0.00)
flatten likewise. Nine of ten models show an out-of-domain delta not distinguishable from zero, and
the us-closed class mean falls from +0.572 on civil liberties to +0.250 out of domain — a residual
carried almost entirely by Opus.

| Model | Civil-liberties Δ(A→B) | Out-of-domain Δ(A→B) [95% CI] |
|-------|----------------------:|------------------------------:|
| Claude Opus 4.7 | +0.90 | **+0.50 [+0.12, +0.88]** — significant |
| Grok 4.3 | +0.90 | +0.00 [−0.62, +0.50] |
| GPT-4.1 | +0.43 | −0.12 [−0.38, +0.00] |
| Mistral Large | +0.30 | +0.00 [+0.00, +0.00] |
| Gemini 2.5 Pro | +0.06 | +0.62 [−0.25, +1.50] — not sig. |
| DeepSeek V3.2 / Llama-4 / Gemma-2-27b / Gemma-3-27b / GLM-4.7 | ≈0 | ≈0 |

Two readings, both of which *strengthen* the study's scope discipline rather than weaken the
headline. First, the construct is **substantially civil-liberties-specific**: the unmask fires on
state-power / speech / surveillance framing and does not transfer to "is NATO expansion stabilizing"
or "should the Fed be independent," where even the strong unmaskers default to balanced. This is
direct evidence that the scope limitation stated in the abstract and at the top of §5.4 is the
*correct* claim boundary, not merely a cautious one. Second, **Opus is the lone exception** — its
institutional skepticism is broad enough to bleed across domains, consistent with its being the
version-arc leader (§3.2). This is an exploratory probe (n = 8 questions, one framing, one sample per
cell; CIs are correspondingly wide) reported under the same CI-excludes-zero rule as the rest of the
study; the run ships in `data/2026-05-27-ood/`.

**Paraphrase-robustness control (wording sensitivity).** The remaining construct objection is
that the unmask might be keyed to the *exact wording* of the ten neutral questions — a model
triggering memorized talking points on specific phrasing rather than expressing a stable
disposition. We rewrote each of the ten neutrals three independent ways (`questions.md`
`para1`/`para2`/`para3`), same institutional pole, and re-ran the A→B unmask under the same
four-judge panel on the four FDR unmaskers plus two flat controls (`data/2026-05-27-paraphrase/`).
The effect holds where it is large: the two models with a substantial neutral-framing unmask
reproduce it across all three rewordings — **Grok 4.3 at +0.90 / +0.60 / +0.70 against an
original-neutral +0.80, Opus 4.7 at +0.70 / +0.80 / +0.90 against +0.60** — and the flat controls
(Mistral Large, Gemma-2-27b) stay flat under every rewording. GPT-4.1's neutral-subset effect is
small to begin with (+0.10) and scatters around zero across the paraphrases (+0.10 / −0.30 / 0.00),
consistent with a small effect rather than a wording artifact of a large one. The load-bearing
unmasks are a disposition, not memorized phrasing keyed to a particular sentence.

### 5.5 Open vs closed models — and the transparency-asymmetry

Open-weight models show framing-stability under the prompt rung (Δ ≈ 0). The §4.2 weight-rung
experiment was built to ask why — and it was **executed this run**, not deferred. Abliterating the
refusal direction from four open-weight families removes refusals and heavily rewrites the
political text but leaves the stance flat (§4.2). So the open-model prompt-flatness is not simply
"so thoroughly aligned the instruction adds nothing" — the refusal mechanism can be removed
outright and the stance still does not move. Whatever encodes the institutional prior, it is not
the refusal direction abliteration removes.

The structural point is an **auditability asymmetry**, stated carefully to avoid overclaiming. We
do *not* claim "open models lean less *because* they are open" — open models are smaller and
differently trained, so a capability/training confound cannot be excluded and that causal claim
would be unfalsifiable here. The defensible claim is narrower: **weight-level verification
(abliteration) is only possible on open weights.** The US-closed frontier models that show the
largest prompt-rung unmask (§3.1, §4.1) cannot be abliterated at all — there are no weights to
operate on. The strongest probe in the toolkit applies precisely where the measured prompt-lean is
smallest and cannot apply where it is largest. That is an accountability gap independent of which
way any given closed model leans — and note we *retracted* the GPT-5 lean claim (§3.3); we assert
only that the closed frontier is structurally un-auditable from the outside, not that it is "the problem."

*Toolchain note.* A first attempt against a 14B model at 8-bit hit a clean wall — **you cannot
abliterate a quantized model** (the projection needs real fp16 weights); a 24 GB GPU caps this at
~7–9B at fp16, which is what §4.2 uses. The OBLITERATUS `aggressive` preset and the Gemma-2 family
currently fail an `linalg.eigh`/MKL `SSYEVD` backend bug, so ablation strength is raised on the
working SVD path (more directions) rather than via that preset.

### 5.6 Multiple comparisons

With ~13 per-model tests in the main run, some CI-excluding-zero deltas are expected by chance. We apply a Benjamini-Hochberg FDR correction (q = 0.05) over the per-model bootstrap p-values (`scripts/robustness_checks.py`). **Four of thirteen effects survive FDR**: Claude Opus 4.7, Grok 4.3, GPT-4.1, and Mistral Large (all bootstrap p ≈ 0). DeepSeek V3.2 (raw p = 0.039) survives the per-model CI but **not** the multiple-comparisons correction, so we demote it to "suggestive, not confirmed." Per-topic and per-cell extremes (e.g. a single model's +2.00 on one topic) are exploratory and not multiple-comparisons-controlled; they are reported as observations, not tested claims.

### 5.7 Length / verbosity control

A reviewer will ask whether the unmask effect is a length artifact — the score-3 "balanced essay" is long, and LLM judges can exhibit verbosity bias. It is not. Across the main run, score correlates only modestly with response length (Pearson r = +0.22, score vs. `word_count_total`), and critically the **unmasked (B) responses are *shorter* than the fairness-prompted (A) ones — mean 385 vs 441 words** — yet score *higher* on skepticism. If length drove the score, the longer A responses would score higher; they don't. The unmask delta runs opposite to the length gradient, so it is not a verbosity artifact.

### 5.8 Judge-method robustness (the C3 contamination measurement)

The most persistent reviewer attack on LLM-as-judge studies: the judges share RLHF alignment with the systems under test, so any cross-judge consensus launders the very prior the instrument claims to measure. The ULTRAPLINIAN-4 cross-vendor median provides robustness to *one* outlier judge but does not, on its own, escape a *shared* prior carried by all four panel members.

We convert that objection into a measurement. The entire pre-registered dataset (seven runs, 53 raw model-files, ≈ 1,780 records) was re-scored under five alternative judging procedures, each designed to break the shared-prior hypothesis in a distinct way:

| # | Method | Mechanism that breaks the shared-prior hypothesis |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | ULTRAPLINIAN-4 cross-vendor median (baseline) | The instrument under test |
| 2 | Abliterated Gemma-2-9B-IT (MLX, in-process) | Refusal direction surgically removed at the weight level via the Arditi-et-al-2024 procedure (`OBLITERATUS`); the judge no longer carries an intact RLHF refusal mechanism |
| 4 | Grok-solo (single low-RLHF judge) | One bias replaces four; sensitivity check |
| 5 | Adversarial-pair (single base judge, opposing-instructed prompts) | Symmetric-prior cancellation at the pair mean |
| 6 | Reversed-rubric (same ULTRAPLINIAN panel, labels inverted 1↔5) | Direct test of anchor-position bias vs content-bias |
| 7 | Blind-condition (same panel, condition + question-framing redacted) | Forces scoring on response text alone; removes condition-aware confounding |

The methodology-quality rubric (`RUBRIC-SCORES.md`, weights 0.30 / 0.25 / 0.20 / 0.15 / 0.10 on circularity-reduction / construct-validity / reproducibility / interpretability / cost) was committed to the repository on 2026-05-28, *before* any cross-method sweep result existed in the git history; the resulting anchor selection — **Method 2 (abliterated-gemma), rubric total 4.10** — is therefore mechanically determined rather than post-hoc chosen. The 2026-05-28 → 2026-05-29 API sweep populated Methods 4–7 on the 4090 PC; the 2026-05-30 M5 MLX sweep populated Method 2; the complete 6 × 7 matrix is verifiable at any time via `scripts/sweep_status.py`.

**Cross-method agreement against the ULTRAPLINIAN-4 baseline** (pooled across all 7 pre-registered runs):

| Alternative method | Paired records | Exact match | Within one ordinal step |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Reversed-rubric (M6)    | 1,743 | **90.7%** | 99.1% |
| Blind-condition (M7)    | 1,713 | 88.5% | 98.9% |
| Grok-solo (M4)          | 1,690 | 87.7% | 99.2% |
| Abliterated-gemma (M2)  | 1,717 | 86.8% | 99.2% |
| Adversarial-pair (M5)   | 1,652 | 84.3% | 98.4% |

Every alternative method agrees with the cross-vendor median on the exact 1–5 score for **84–91% of records** and within one ordinal step at **98.4–99.2%**. Two findings deserve emphasis:

First, the **reversed-rubric** method — the same ULTRAPLINIAN panel with the rubric labels flipped 1↔5 before being shown to the judge, with the returned scores algorithmically un-flipped during aggregation — is the *highest*-agreeing method, at 90.7% exact-match. If the panel were scoring by anchor position rather than by response content, label inversion would have produced systematic divergence. It produced the strongest convergence instead. The score reflects content, not the scale position the panel was anchored on.

Second, the **abliterated open-weight judge** — Gemma-2-9B-IT with the refusal direction projected out of its weights, MLX-converted for in-process Apple-Silicon inference — agrees with the four-judge RLHF-aligned panel at 86.8% exact-match, between blind-condition and grok-solo. The judge that mechanically does not carry the prior the reviewer is concerned about produces the same score as the panel that does. The shared-RLHF-prior hypothesis is not supported by the data.

**Per-model contamination delta against the pre-registered 0.10 bound.** The locked decision rule (`RUBRIC-SCORES.md`): if `median |Δ per-model vs ULTRAPLINIAN-4|` ≤ 0.10 across all model-run pairs, the original consensus is robust to that bias-source. Across all 7 runs (47 model × run pairs), the contamination delta has median **0.062** and mean **0.101**, with **68.1% of model-runs at delta ≤ 0.10**. The median falls inside the pre-registered robust band. The mean is pulled to the threshold by small-sample outliers (e.g. google/gemini-2.5-pro in the primary run, n=27, delta 0.481); the pre-registration named the median rather than the mean to absorb exactly this kind of high-variance edge case. The §3 and §4 headline deltas are robust to judge-alignment contamination as the pre-registration defined "robust."

Methods 4–7 are reported as sensitivity analyses in this section, not demoted. The choice of anchor moves the headline numbers by at most one ordinal step on any given record.

**Addendum (2026-06-10) — the abliterated judge, characterized directly.** Method 2's abliterated Gemma-2-9B-IT is the spine of a dedicated follow-on instrument, *The Wash*, which validates the abliterated-judge approach on its own terms rather than only by cross-method agreement. Three results bear on its use here. (1) A **dose-response** locates the usable abliteration band: a coherence cliff below `n_dir=2` (perplexity → ∞, coherence 0.0, reproduced 3×) and a stable band at `n_dir ∈ {2,4,8}` (perplexity 6.9–7.9, coherence 1.0) — an abliterated judge is valid only inside a verified-coherent regime, which the Method-2 spine occupies. (2) A **refusal/flinch decoupling** (underpowered — overlapping CIs, n=30/dose): across that band the judge's residual false-flag (flinch) rate (0.067–0.138) shows no detectable co-movement with its refusal rate (0.167–0.300); suggestive of dissociable axes, not yet established. (3) A **per-move observation** — *demoted on adversarial review (The Wash §F)*: on a single `documented-exposure` template, two of three aligned families (Gemma-2-27B, Llama-3.3-70B) over-flag a rubric-PASS attributed/public-record construction at 1.00 where the abliterated spine flags 0.00, but Qwen-2.5-72B does not reproduce it (0.20) — one template, two of three families. The 2026-06-11 multi-template re-run **confirmed** this in refined, CI-backed form: pooled per-sample Wilson CIs across a 16-template battery × 5 families show controlled judges over-flag documented institutional criticism the spine passes — single facts 0.16 [0.13,0.21] vs spine 0.00, juxtaposed 0.55 [0.49,0.61] vs spine 0.31 (gaps disjoint), with the spine validated as a discriminator (smuggle-controls 1.00, clean 0.00). The single-template "1.00" overstated the magnitude; the real gap is +0.16–0.24 (The Wash §F). The corollary for this study is unaffected by that demotion: the controlled-judge prior §5.8 controls for plausibly concentrates on the documented/attributed register — the register a contested-institution answer occupies — so an abliterated Method-2 anchor is a sensible control, and what carries weight here is the measured **86.8% agreement** with the RLHF panel, not the Wash's spine-vs-controlled gap (whose spine baseline the review flags as gate-defined rather than independently validated). Full methods, tables, and CIs: [`results/THE-WASH-2026-06-10.md`](THE-WASH-2026-06-10.md).

**Reproducibility.** Per-run cross-method outputs at `runs/<date>/cross-method/{contamination-delta,cross-method-agreement,per-method-summary,per-topic-disagreement}.json`; aggregated index at `runs/_aggregated/cross-method-runs-index.json`; chart assets regenerated from the JSON at `website/static/images/bias-study/{forest-plot-per-model,agreement-heatmap,contamination-delta,paraphrase-robustness}.png`. ## 7. Headline conclusions

1. **The hedge is the bias signature.** Score-3 (balanced) responses carry 7× the hedge ratio of score-1 or score-5 responses. The "balanced essay" mode is observable, quantifiable, and consistent across vendors.

2. **US-closed frontier models unmask further than any other vendor class.** US-closed mean Δ = +0.572 vs. open-weight ≈ 0 and chinese-closed ≈ +0.08. The fairness layer is heaviest where alignment training is most aggressive.

3. **Anthropic Opus drifts upward across versions; OpenAI's signal is mixed.** Anthropic Opus 4.0 → 4.7 trends upward — every version's unmask is significant (CI excludes zero), reaching +0.90 [0.67, 1.13] at 4.7, ~3× the 4.0 baseline (an upward trend; 4.5 wobbles within noise, not strict monotonicity). GPT-4.1 unmasks significantly (+0.43 [0.20, 0.70]); GPT-5 does **not** — its delta is not distinguishable from zero and it is the study's noisiest model (σ=1.14). The earlier "GPT-4.1→GPT-5 reversal" is not supported: GPT-5 is indeterminate, not reversed.

4. **Under sufficient unmask pressure, the v1 magnitude (+2.00) is reproducible — but the locus moved.** Grok 4.3 hits 5.00 across all questions under the opinionated-commentator persona. The phenomenon is general; the v1 attribution to Gemma 2 was one realization. GPT-5 is the exception only in the sense that it shows no consistent movement under any prompt strength — but given its σ=1.14 variance, that is better read as high-noise indeterminacy than as genuine dose-insensitivity.

5. **Encryption-backdoors is the topic of broadest cross-vendor institutional skepticism.** Six frontier models from four vendors / three jurisdictions all unmask positive on T03. AI models broadly agree the institutional position on encryption backdoors is wrong.

6. **The institutional lean is not the refusal direction (weight rung).** Abliterating the refusal direction from four open-weight families removes refusals and rewrites ~70% of the political wording, yet the institutional-skepticism stance moves ≤0.2 (§4.2). The two are dissociable. And because abliteration needs the weights, this deepest check reaches only open models (the low-lean ones) and never the US-closed frontier — the transparency-asymmetry: the public can audit at the weights only the models the prompt rung does *not* flag.

7. **The protocol is the artifact.** Single runs at temperature 0.7 have a ≈±0.5 noise floor (mean σ 0.53; §5.1). The findings that survive that noise — reported here with CI-excluding-zero status — are the Anthropic Opus upward arc, the large per-model unmasks (Opus 4.7, Grok 4.3, GPT-4.1, Mistral, DeepSeek), the vendor-class direction (replicated under N=5 averaging), and the hedge-ratio signature. Claims that do NOT survive — the GPT-5 "reversal," and the long tail of near-zero per-model deltas — are labeled as such. The protocol exists to be re-run quarterly and to update the public record as model versions change.

## 8. Future work

- **Pipeline rung (G0DM0D3).** The middle rung of the escalation ladder (§4) — hedge-strip + layered obfuscation via the G0DM0D3 STM/Parseltongue server — is specified but not yet executed. It sits between prompt-unmask (§4.1) and weight-abliteration (§4.2) and would test whether elicitation-layer force moves the *stance* where prompt force alone does not. Layered obfuscation is most effective up to a functionality ceiling; a coherence guard (a broken response must never be scored as "maximum skepticism") is a design requirement, and the ceiling itself is a measurable result.
- **Closed-model red-team elicitation.** Weight abliteration cannot reach the US-closed frontier models — no weights (§4.2, §5.5). Constraint-free red-team API access (if granted by the labs) is the only lever that reaches those models, letting us push them to their no-refusal elicitation ceiling and test whether political lean intensifies when the official content layer is lifted — partially closing the transparency-asymmetry.
- **Wider abliteration breadth.** Gemma-2 and the `aggressive`-strength preset currently fail an eigendecomposition backend bug (`linalg.eigh` / MKL `SSYEVD`; §4.2) — strength is instead increased on the working SVD path (more directions). A 14B+ family needs a unified-memory box (the 4090 caps ~7–9B at fp16).
- Both companion construct controls are **done** (§5.4): the **out-of-domain** test (the lean does not transfer beyond the civil-liberties surface for any model except Opus 4.7) and the **paraphrase-robustness** test (the large unmasks reproduce across three independent rewordings; the effect is a disposition, not memorized phrasing). The remaining open work is the standing quarterly re-run as model versions ship, and extending the weight rung to 14B+ models on unified-memory hardware.
- **Quarterly cadence (the barometer).** The protocol is built to re-run every quarter as model versions ship; an orchestration agent runs the full ladder and diffs against the prior quarter, maintaining a longitudinal public record of model-bias drift.

## 10a. Erratum (2026-08-25) — bootstrap intervals were argument-order dependent

The intervals published above were computed with `random.seed(20260527)` set once at
module import, so every bootstrap drew sequentially from **one shared generator**. The
draws a given (run, model) cell received therefore depended on how many cells had been
processed before it — which is to say, on the order the run-dates happened to be listed
on the command line.

Measured, not inferred: passing the five analysed run-dates in reverse order moved
**10 of 46 published CI cells**. Changing the seed moved a comparable number. No verdict
flipped at any point, but the study's own reporting rule is binary — a delta is a finding
only when its CI excludes zero — so a bound sitting a few hundredths from zero was
decidable by argument order. That is a reproducibility defect regardless of whether it
happened to change a conclusion here.

**The fix.** `scripts/studypaths.py` derives an independent generator per cell from the
declared seed and the cell's own key, so a cell now depends only on its own data.
`scripts/selftest_analysis.py` gate **G4** asserts byte-identical output across three
argument orders; it fails on the old code, which is how the fix is known to be real.

**What moved.** Re-running the five analysed runs under the fix moves **12 of 46 cells**,
all of them by ≤0.13 on one bound, and **no verdict changes**:

**Why the numbers above are not simply edited.** The anti-misuse rules in §5 say a defect
stays in the run that had it, flagged, rather than being quietly rewritten to look
correct. So the body of this writeup keeps what was published and this section records
what changed and why. The corrected values are what `scripts/ci_analysis.py` produces
today, and `selftest_analysis.py` G1 asserts them as exact equalities so a *further*
drift cannot hide behind this one.

**Seed policy going forward.** May's `20260527` stays frozen as the fallback when a run's
`manifest.json` declares no `analysis_seed`, which keeps the table above reproducible.
Every new run declares its own seed, and `run_study.py` writes it into the manifest **at
run start, before any response exists** — a seed chosen after the intervals are visible is
a researcher degree of freedom, and pinning it beforehand forecloses that mechanically,
the same way the rubric is committed before the sweep.
