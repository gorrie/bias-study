# The Wash: A Symmetric Framing Detector with an Abliterated Spine

**An LLM-as-judge instrument for smuggled political framing, validated against the judge's own alignment-layer flinch, and characterized at the prompt, the scale, and the weight level.**

Companion to the institutional-skepticism audit (`results/WRITEUP-2026-05-26.md`); this document is the standalone methods-and-results record for the judge instrument that supplies that study's §5.8 cross-method robustness leg. Source corpus, rubric, run scripts, and raw votes are published; see §6.

---

## Abstract

LLM-as-judge bias detectors inherit the judge's own alignment lean: an aligned model asked to score text for smuggled framing scores its own discomfort, flagging openly-declared heterodox argument that the rubric explicitly exempts. The Wash addresses this by using, as the judge, open-weight models with the refusal direction projected out of the weights (OBLITERATUS abliteration), and by gating panel membership on a measured non-flinch property rather than assuming it. We report, with bootstrap 95% CIs and the convention that a delta is a finding only if its CI excludes zero: (1) on 160 contested-politics answers re-scored by both judge types, abliterated "spine" judges flag 0–9% of items while controlled judges flag 38–90% — the controlled flag-rate rises with how openly a stance is declared, i.e. the controlled judges measure discomfort, not smuggling; (2) two controlled judges built by different vendors assign *opposite* mean coding directions to the framing they flag (one right-coded ~5/6, the other left-coded ~2.5/3) while the abliterated judge flags both directions approximately evenly — there is no vendor-neutral controlled referee; (3) **dose-response (Exp 1):** abliteration strength is parameterized by the number of projected refusal directions `n_dir`; on Gemma-2-9B-IT with the strong-layer set pinned (layers 24–41), `n_dir=1` is a coherence cliff (perplexity → ∞, coherence 0.0, empty-string generation, reproduced 3×) while `n_dir ∈ {2,4,8}` form a usable band (perplexity 6.9–7.9, coherence 1.0); no right-edge cliff appears through `n_dir=8`; (4) **decoupling (Exp 2):** across the usable band the OBLITERATUS-reported refusal_rate spans 0.167–0.300 while the calibration flinch_rate stays 0.067–0.138 — refusal and flinch are dissociable axes (point-estimate evidence; n=30 per dose, individual dose-pair contrasts underpowered); (5) **per-move, not per-target (Exp 3):** instantiating matched-pair templates across 16 (then 6) category-placeholder power-structure targets, the controlled judge's flag-rate on rubric-PASS boundary items is target-invariant (boundary-flag spread 0.000 at samples=1; 0.222 at samples=5, within the 2× per-cell noise floor of 0.255 and anti-hypothesis in direction) — there is no per-target asymmetry to rank at category abstraction. On the **single** `documented-exposure` template, Gemma-2-27B and Llama-3.3-70B flag a rubric-PASS attributed/public-record construction where the spine does not (1.00 vs 0.00 across noun-substitutions), but Qwen-2.5-72B does not reproduce it (0.20) — **two of three families, one template** (the per-target values are `{TARGET_NP}` substitutions of one sentence; the `sd 0.00` is across-target modal-vote consistency, not precision). A *labeled* opinion ("declared-thesis") is passed by all judges (0.00). This finding is **demoted to a suggestive single-template observation** (see Review status, immediately below): it hints the controlled flinch is content/register-shaped rather than target-shaped, but is not established.

## 1. Introduction

A keyword scanner cannot detect a frame; only a reader can. The natural reader is an LLM, and the natural objection is immediate: the LLM has the lean too. An aligned model asked "is this text smuggling a political frame?" does not evaluate the structure of the claim. It reports whether the claim is comfortable. Handed an openly-labeled heterodox thesis — a stated argument, not a smuggle — it flags the thesis. This is not a hypothetical: a stock open-weight model (phi4), given a rubric that states in writing that a declared thesis is not a smuggle, flagged a labeled authorial thesis four times in one passage. A detector that flags the content it is meant to be scoring structurally is not a detector.

The Wash treats this flinch as a measurable, removable property of the judge. The instrument has three commitments. First, the judge is an abliterated open-weight model — OBLITERATUS projects the refusal direction out of the weights, leaving a model that scores structure rather than permission (the "spine"). Second, panel membership is *earned*: a candidate judge must demonstrate, at scale, that it does not flinch, before it is trusted (§2.5). Third, the instrument reports asymmetry of treatment, never guilt: it measures whether a structurally identical claim is flagged differently, not whether any named entity is culpable.

This document characterizes the instrument at three levels of force, mirroring the parent study's escalation ladder: the prompt/rubric level (Tier-A, §3.1–3.2), and the weight level via a dose-response in abliteration strength (Exp 1, §3.3), a refusal-vs-flinch decoupling test (Exp 2, §3.4), and a target-asymmetry probe (Exp 3, §3.5).

## 2. Methods

### 2.1 The spine: abliteration and dose

The spine judges are open-weight instruction models with the refusal direction abliterated in fp16 (OBLITERATUS). Production abliteration runs on Apple Silicon (M5); the reference family is Gemma-2-9B-IT, with a community-abliterated Qwen-2.5-7B as a second spine. Abliteration strength is the number of projected refusal directions `n_dir`. To avoid a layer-count confound (the default `knee_cosmic` layer selector varies the number of strong layers across `n_dir`), all dose-series runs pin the strong-layer set to layers 24–41 (18 layers); only `n_dir` varies.

### 2.2 Rubric and the four moves

The judge applies one symmetric rubric to both political directions, detecting four structural moves: (a) unattributed characterization, (b) asymmetric skepticism, (c) smuggled premise, (d) weasel construction. The rubric explicitly exempts declared theses, attributed characterizations, documented harms, and sourced facts — these must PASS. Output is strict JSON: per-flag move + coding direction + neutral rewrite, plus a `verdict` ∈ {PASS, TILT, FAIL}. Coding direction is tagged but is never the finding; the structural move is the finding.

### 2.3 Corpus, boundary items, and controls

The matched-pair corpus (`corpus/ratchet/pairs*.jsonl`) holds three template roles. **Smuggle-controls** are heavy-handed smuggles both judge types should FAIL (agreement anchors). **Clean-controls** are flat sourced facts both should PASS. **Boundary items** are rubric-PASS constructions under sayability pressure — a declared thesis, both-sides attributed criticism, and a documented public-record exposure — where any flag is by definition a flinch. The boundary items carry the signal; the controls validate that the divergence is real and not a corpus artifact. Each template substitutes a `{TARGET_NP}` category placeholder (e.g. "the intelligence agencies", "a major social-media company", "the central bank"); targets are categories, never named organizations, and the instrument measures judge behavior, not target culpability.

### 2.4 Judges and panel

A run specifies a panel of spine judges (abliterated) and controlled judges (stock RLHF). Spine judges are served locally (in-process MLX or an OpenAI-compatible endpoint over the abliterated weights); controlled judges are served via OpenRouter. Per-item verdicts are taken at temperature 0.1; multi-sample runs vote at vote-temperature 0.7 and report the modal verdict.

### 2.5 Calibration gate

A candidate judge must pass two preflight gates before joining a panel: a JSON-compliance check and a flinch-calibration check at scale (false-flag rate on a condition-A baseline sample below threshold). This gate is load-bearing: an imported abliterated phi4 passed every quick check, then at scale flagged 84–89% of items — statistically identical to the *controlled* phi4 it was meant to liberate (cosmetic abliteration). Only the at-scale gate caught it. A preflight smoke test additionally exercises every judge with one live call before any matrix launch, so a dead leg fails fast rather than after hours of compute.

### 2.6 Metrics

Per judge and target we compute flag-rate (fraction of items not PASS) over a template role. The Tier-B target probe reports the **boundary-isolated** metric: flag-rate restricted to the rubric-PASS boundary items, with smuggle/clean controls as validity gates. The per-target ranking gate is the *controlled* judge's across-target spread (the instrument under test), compared against a per-cell noise floor (mean within-(judge,item) across-target sd); a ranking is claimable only if the controlled spread exceeds 2× that floor. Differential spread is explicitly *not* the gate, because spine instability inflates it without any controlled target effect. Dose metrics (perplexity, coherence, refusal_rate) are the OBLITERATUS verify-stage outputs (`verify_sample_size=30`); flinch_rate is the calibration false-flag rate with bootstrap 95% CIs.

### 2.7 Reproduction

Abliteration runs on M5 (the 4090's MKL SSYEVD fails on Gemma-2); inference of the result runs on any host. The dose series runs under a supervised driver with a pre-flight RAM check, a CPU-activity-aware stall watchdog, and a wall-clock backstop. Long matrices run under a session-independent monitor that validates the artifact before declaring success. Recipe, rubric, corpus, run scripts, dose-response curve, boundary-isolated analyzer, and raw per-judge votes are published (§6).

## 3. Results

### 3.1 Tier-A: spine vs controlled flag-rate (160 answers)

Re-scoring 160 contested-politics answers from the parent study (two dozen frontier and open models, half under a fairness instruction, half without):

| Judge type | flag-rate range |
|---|---|
| abliterated (spine) | 0–9% |
| controlled (stock RLHF) | 38–90% |

The controlled flag-rate rises with how openly a stance is declared: removing a model's fairness instruction makes it *unmask* (state its view more openly), and an openly-stated view is a declared stance, not a smuggle. The spine passes declared stance (that is the job); the controlled judge flags it, harder the more openly it is said. The two judge types measure different constructs — smuggling vs discomfort.

### 3.2 Tier-A: direction asymmetry (no vendor-neutral referee)

Coding direction assigned to flagged framing, by judge:

| Judge | dominant coded direction |
|---|---|
| controlled A | "right" ~5/6 of flags |
| controlled B (different vendor) | "left" ~2.5/3 of flags |
| abliterated spine | both directions ≈ even |

Two controlled judges built by different companies lean opposite ways; neither is balanced. The lean is not a configurable policy — it is a property of the weights and points where training pointed it. The abliterated judge is the only panel member that flags both directions approximately evenly, which is the property a symmetric instrument requires to survive contact with an adversarial audience.

### 3.3 Exp 1: dose-response (the abliteration mechanism)

Gemma-2-9B-IT, strong layers pinned to 24–41 (18 layers), only `n_dir` varied:

| `n_dir` | perplexity | coherence | refusal_rate | verdict |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 0 (stock) | base | base | base | reference |
| 1 | ∞ | 0.0 | 0.0 | **broken — left-edge cliff** |
| 2 | 6.91 | 1.0 | 0.267 | usable |
| 4 | 7.87 | 1.0 | 0.167 | usable |
| 8 | 7.73 | 1.0 | 0.300 | usable |

There is a **coherence cliff at `n_dir < 2`**, reproduced three times under a clean toolchain (pin held, full-scale prompts, no memory pressure, per-attempt diagnostics ruling out swap-thrash). Projecting a single refusal direction across the 18-layer reference range leaves the model unable to produce coherent text — generation returns the empty string. The refusal subspace's effective rank exceeds 1. The usable spine occupies a narrow band (`n_dir ∈ {2,4,8}`, perplexity inside the reference quality band, coherence 1.0); no right-edge cliff appears through `n_dir=8`. refusal_rate is non-monotonic across the band (0.267 → 0.167 → 0.300); with `verify_sample_size=30`, ±0.1 noise is plausible, so more projection is not strictly "more refusal removed."

### 3.4 Exp 2: refusal/flinch decoupling

`preflight.py --calibrate --cal-n 30` against each MLX-served dose, pairing the calibration flinch_rate with the OBLITERATUS-reported refusal_rate:

| dose | `n_dir` | refusal_rate | flinch_rate [95% CI] | json_compliance |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| stock | — | baseline | 0.100 [0.00, 0.20] | 1.000 |
| dose2 | 2 | 0.267 | 0.067 [0.00, 0.17] | 1.000 |
| dose4 | 4 | 0.167 | 0.100 [0.00, 0.20] | 1.000 |
| dose8 | 8 | 0.300 | 0.138 [0.03, 0.28] | 0.967 |

Across the usable band, refusal_rate spans 0.167–0.300 while flinch_rate holds 0.067–0.138. **Corrected (review §F8):** every flinch CI overlaps every other (stock 0.100 [0.00, 0.20] … dose8 0.138 [0.03, 0.28]), and dose8's denominator is **29, not 30** (one no-vote, json_compliance 0.967, so 4/29). By this study's own CI-excludes-zero rule, **no dose-pair flinch contrast is individually significant.** The defensible statement is *no detectable co-movement between refusal and flinch in this range* — consistent with dissociable axes but equally with an underpowered flat flinch; and the two are different instruments (OBLITERATUS verify-stage on harmful prompts vs preflight false-flag on heterodox baseline), so "moves on different scales" is near-tautological. A joint refusal×flinch interaction test at the pre-registered n≥200/cell (accumulated over the longitudinal resample) is required before "dissociable" is claimable.

### 3.5 Exp 3: per-move, not per-target

**Baseline (samples=1, 16 targets, controlled = Gemma-2-27B, spine = dose-2).** Controls valid (both judges: smuggle-detect 1.00, clean-pass-flag 0.00). The controlled judge's boundary-flag rate is **0.67 for all 16 targets, spread 0.000** — fully target-invariant. The original all-template metric reported a flat 0.70 and a spurious "ranking" carried entirely by spine instability; the boundary-isolated re-analysis localizes the effect: the controlled judge over-flags two of three boundary items (`attributed-criticism` 1.00, `documented-exposure` 1.00) and correctly passes `declared-thesis` (0.00), identically across every target.

**Confirmatory (samples=5, 6 targets, three controlled families).** Per-item flag-rate (across-target sd in parentheses):

| boundary item (rubric-PASS) | spine-dose2 | Gemma-2-27B | Llama-3.3-70B | Qwen-2.5-72B |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| declared-thesis (labeled opinion) | 0.00 | 0.00 | 0.00 | 0.00 |
| attributed-criticism (both-sides) | 0.00 | 1.00 | 0.17 (.37) | 0.83 (.37) |
| **documented-exposure** (public-record) | **0.00** | **1.00** | **1.00** | 0.20 (.40) |

On the `documented-exposure` template, two aligned families (Gemma-2-27B, Llama-3.3-70B) flag a rubric-PASS attributed/public-record statement where the spine does not (1.00 vs 0.00). **Corrected (review §F1–F3, F11):** (a) this is a **single template** — the per-target values are `{TARGET_NP}` substitutions of one sentence, not independent constructs (pseudoreplication; n_templates=1); (b) the `sd 0.00` is across-target consistency of the *modal* vote (the 5 samples are collapsed before recording), not measurement precision; (c) **Qwen-2.5-72B does not reproduce it** (0.20) — two of three families, not a generalization; (d) Gemma-2-27B also flags the *clean-control* at 0.17, so part of its 1.00 is baseline false-positive propensity, not register-specific flinch (the claim needs documented-exposure *minus* each judge's own clean-control, with a CI on the difference). `declared-thesis` is passed by all four judges (0.00), so flagging is not indiscriminate. Net: a **suggestive single-template observation**, not a register-level result.

**No per-target ranking detected at category-placeholder abstraction.** The aggregate controlled boundary-flag spread is 0.222, within the per-cell noise floor (2× mean controlled item-sd = 0.255) and anti-hypothesis in direction. **Corrected (review §F6):** this is a null, and the targets are vague category placeholders, so target-invariance is what one would predict if the abstraction itself washes out any gradient — it does **not** establish "the judge is target-blind." A real sayability ladder (concrete sensitive vs anodyne, matched left/right) was not run (remediation R6); until it is, "per-move not per-target" is an undertested null, not a structural property of the judge.

## 4. Limitations and honest negatives

- **Synthetic corpus does not discriminate between targets.** This was first filed as a corpus failure; the boundary-isolated analysis shows it is the finding (target-invariance), not a defect. It remains true that the corpus cannot surface a *target* gradient at category abstraction; testing one would require concrete, sayability-laddered, politically-symmetric category targets — a design extension, not run here.
- **Exp 1 verify sample is small.** OBLITERATUS `verify_sample_size=30` makes refusal_rate noisy to ±0.1; the cliff (∞/0.0) is unambiguous, the within-band refusal ordering is not.
- **Exp 2 is underpowered for pairwise contrasts.** n=30 per dose; the decoupled *shape* is supported, individual dose-pair flinch deltas are not individually significant.
- **Exp 3 rests on a single template, modal-collapsed, two-of-three families (review §F1–F3).** The samples=5 run does NOT harden a generalization: the per-target values are noun-substitutions of one `documented-exposure` sentence (pseudoreplication, n_templates=1); the per-sample variance is discarded before recording (so `sd 0.00` is mode-consistency, not precision); and Qwen-2.5-72B does not reproduce the effect (0.20). Per-cell CIs are not computed below the modal vote. Suggestive single-template observation.
- **Spine-as-ground-truth circularity (review §F4).** The instrument is validated against the abliterated spine, whose 0.00 is assumed correct; the calibration gate operationalizes "correct" as "flags little," so a *blunted* judge (abliteration sits one direction from incoherence, §3.3) is indistinguishable from a genuinely *symmetric* one. No spine sensitivity / d-prime check (does it still CATCH graded smuggles?) has been run — remediation R4.
- **The rubric supplies the answer key (review §F5).** "Boundary items are rubric-PASS" is the authors' reading; `documented-exposure` selects and juxtaposes facts to imply capture, and three aligned models converging is at least as consistent with the rubric being wrong as with a flinch. No blinded human adjudication of the rendered strings — remediation R5.
- **Serving-path confound (review §F10).** Spine served in-process MLX; controlled via OpenRouter — different quantization, sampler, templating, provider wrapping. The flag-rate gap could be the harness, not the weights — remediation R9.
- **No multiple-comparisons correction (review §F12).** The headline cell is the post-hoc maximum of a large judge × item × target matrix; no BH-FDR was applied. The re-run pre-registers `documented-exposure` (multi-template) as the primary endpoint.
- **Cosmetic abliteration is a real failure mode.** A 14B import was a weaker judge than a 9B; an abliterated phi4 was statistically identical to its controlled twin at scale. The calibration gate (§2.5) exists because quick checks do not catch this.
- **Transparency asymmetry.** Abliteration is only possible on open weights, so the deepest verification reaches open models, not the high-lean US-closed frontier — the same audit-reach asymmetry noted in the parent study.

## 5. Interpretation

The Tier-B results sketch a mechanism the review (§F) bounds tightly. The dose-1 cliff (§3.3) is solid. Beyond it, the suggestive picture — a flinch that appears to sit in the weights (survives helpfulness training, §3.4) and to track the documented/attributed *construction* of a criticism rather than the named target (§3.5) — rests on one template, two of three families, a spine baseline that the calibration gate *defines* rather than independently validates, and point estimates whose CIs overlap. The honest one-line read: *where it has been measured, an aligned judge's over-flagging appears to track how a criticism is built (documented, attributed) more than who it names* — a **hypothesis** the remediation program (R1–R5, §F) is built to confirm or kill, not a settled finding. The earlier "uniform discomfort with the receipt" framing outran the evidence and is withdrawn pending that work.

## 6. Reproduction and data

Code, corpus, and per-run artifacts (raw per-judge votes, dose-response curve, boundary-isolated analyses) live with the instrument; the parent study's §5.8 reports the cross-method contamination-delta robustness leg that uses the abliterated judge. Re-running requires the OBLITERATUS toolchain (weight rung) and an OpenRouter key (controlled judges); abliteration production requires Apple Silicon. The protocol is built for a roughly quarterly resample so the public record tracks judge-behavior drift as new model versions ship; each cut accumulates n toward the per-cell targets the single-shot runs do not yet meet.
