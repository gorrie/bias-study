# v2 release definition

**Purpose:** state exactly what ships, what does not, and what has to be true before it does —
so the public repository is not a mixture of finished arms, half-collected arms and superseded
claims. Written 2026-09-07.

---

## What v2 is

The forced-choice re-measurement and its floors: **twelve measured resolution objects, the
detection limits derived from them, a twelve-study controls audit, and the wave-0 time-series
baseline.** Plus every correction made to it, in the mirror's `CORRECTIONS.md` (the canonical copy -- this tree deliberately keeps none), including the ones made the
same day they were published.

## RESOLVED 2026-09-07: option B was taken and the collection finished

The re-collection ran and the arm is at n=5 with swept seeds. Result:
[`RESULTS-2026-09-07-ablation-wave.md`](withdrawn/results/RESULTS-2026-09-07-ablation-wave.md), analysed in the
pre-registered order with the stopping rule enforced in code.

**What B delivered, against what it promised:**

| promised | delivered |
|---|---|
| the arm at n=5 instead of n=1 | **yes, and the arm is SPENT rather than complete** — corrected 2026-09-12: 39 of 63 cells at n=5, 0 left to collect, 24 that had their five runs and came back short (`gemma4-12b` stock returns 0 valid of 5 under A). Was written as "28 of 45" mid-collection. `refusal-direction ablation, one sitting` prints beside the old row |
| three independent abliterations of one base | **yes** — `qwen38-27b` × 3, so "model or ablator?" is answerable |
| a re-test of the three exclusions | **yes, and two of the three reproduced** — gemma2-9b still emits tokenizer garbage, llama31-8b still answers in prose |

**And the answer to "is this the model or the ablator?" is: on intensity, the ablator.** Ablator
disagreement reaches 19 endpoint-flips against an endpoint floor of 8, while that base's own
ablation effect reaches 17 — both clear the floor and the disagreement is larger. On direction,
the only base where abliteration measurably moves position is the only base with a single
abliteration, so its effect cannot be separated from that ablator's choices.

That is pre-registered outcome 4, arriving on the endpoint statistic rather than on side-flips.
It is a real finding and it is **not** the finding the arm was hoped to produce, which is why it
was pre-registered.

**The median fell from 6 to 2.** Two-thirds of the older arm's typical "ablation effect" was the
estimator. Same mechanism that took the manipulation floor from a pooled 15 to 7 — wherever this
study measured an effect from single runs, the effect was inflated by noise it could not average
away. Two of three such arms have now been re-collected and both shrank.

**Option A is retrospectively worse than it looked.** Shipping the n=1 row would not merely have
been under-powered; it would have published a 12-item effect that a five-run modal puts at 8–9,
and an "inversion" that does not survive its own re-measurement (CORRECTIONS #8).

The original decision text is kept below, unedited, because a recommendation is only worth
reading next to what happened.

## The one open decision (as written before the collection finished)

`refusal-direction ablation` is **n=1 per cell** — six pairs, two builds, four conditions, one
run each, collected once on 2026-08-30 and never revisited. Three further pairs were *excluded*
on the strength of a single bad run each, and one of those exclusions has already failed to
reproduce at n=5.

It is also the study's most distinctive claim: the only measurement here that no closed lab can
replicate and no API can provide.

**Two options, and the recommendation is B.**

**A — ship it with the n=1 caveat.** Fastest. But an n=1 row inside a paper whose entire
argument is that under-powered numbers should not be published is the paper convicting itself,
and a reviewer will find it in a minute.

**B — hold v2 for the re-collection, which is already running and free.** 6 of 45 cells done,
~10 hours of local GPU remaining, no API spend. It delivers:

- the arm at n=5 with swept seeds instead of n=1
- **three independent abliterations of one base**, so the weight-level effect gets an error bar
  and "is this the model or the ablator?" becomes answerable
- a re-test of the three exclusions rather than three exclusion notes

The cost of B is a day. The cost of A is shipping the exact defect the paper is about.

**Recommendation: B.** If the collection stalls or the ablations disagree wildly, the fallback
is **C — cut the row from v2 entirely** and move the whole arm to v3, which is cleaner than
shipping n=1.

## What is explicitly NOT in v2

Moved to v3 (`BACKLOG-larger-hardware-and-v3.md`), each with its reason:

- **doing the ablation ourselves** — needs bf16, ~54GB for a 27B
- **dose-response on intervention strength** — depends on the above
- **the bf16→Q3 quantisation ladder** — needs bf16 and Q8 resident
- **open-weight models over 24GB** — `qwen3.8-2.4t-a95b`, full-weight DeepSeek/Kimi, `gemma-4-31b-it`
- **many-seed runs on the bimodal cells** — n=25–50 where n=5 cannot separate two modes
- **language / character-set testing** — a translation is a new instrument and needs a
  back-translation control before it yields one honest number

**Moved INTO the v2 workplan after a same-day correction:** *logit scoring*. It was filed as
needing 128GB. It does not — ollama 0.33.1 returns `top_logprobs` at Q4 on the current card,
verified. See "the one thing v2 should still add" below.

## RESOLVED 2026-09-07: logit scoring goes to v3, by the rule written below

Prototyped and measured: [`RESULTS-2026-09-07-logit-scoring-fails-agreement.md`](withdrawn/results/RESULTS-2026-09-07-logit-scoring-fails-agreement.md).
The recommendation below was "prototype it now, publish it in v2 only if the agreement check is
clean, otherwise v3 with the prototype already built." **The agreement check is not clean.**

| check | result |
|---|---|
| capability | real, but on `/v1/chat/completions` — the native `/api/chat` accepts `logprobs` and returns none, silently |
| labels separable at token 1? | **no.** `Strongly Agree` and `Strongly Disagree` both start `'Strong'` — 3 distinct tokens for 4 options, on both models tested |
| letter form separable? | yes, all four letters single tokens, 62 of 62 items scored |
| agrees with the parsed instrument? | **no.** 24 of 62 exact (38.7%), **20 side-flips** against a replicate floor of 5 and an estimator floor of 3 |
| invariant to its own legend? | **no.** Reversing which letter denotes which option changed **48 of 62 positions** |

**And "it is free" was wrong in every sense but API spend.** It is one call per item against one
call per sheet — 62× the requests — it is a second instrument needing its own floors rather than
the parsed one's, and the letter form it forces is not legend-invariant. The four problems below
are real and the solution as described would have traded them for a format whose answers move
when you reorder the answer key.

The prototype is the deliverable: the next attempt starts from a
measured account of why the obvious design fails, and the results document lists the three
things that would have to change. The original text is kept unedited below.

## RESOLVED 2026-09-07: the grammar arm goes to v3 as a working arm, not a rejected one

The same afternoon, the other route to removing the parser was prototyped: **grammar-constrained
decoding**, from Ovando's constitutive-versus-corrective framing
([OSF s9gu6](https://osf.io/s9gu6/overview)). Measured in
[`RESULTS-2026-09-07-constrained-decoding-batch-size.md`](withdrawn/results/RESULTS-2026-09-07-constrained-decoding-batch-size.md).
It reaches a **different verdict from logit scoring, and the difference matters for how v3 is
scoped.**

Where logit scoring failed on *capability* — the labels are not separable at token 1, and the
letter form that is separable is not invariant to its own legend — the grammar arm keeps the
instrument's original wording and fails, at first, on *reliability*. Then it turns out not to
fail at all:

| check | result |
|---|---|
| capability | real. Ollama's `format` takes a JSON schema; 50 runs across 5 models, 62 of 62 answers every time, **zero** parse problems |
| removes the parser's failure classes? | yes, by construction — the 28.2% invalid condition-A runs, the budget exhaustion, the tokenizer garbage |
| agrees with itself? | **not as first built.** All 62 answers in one array: self-spread median **26** against the prose arm's 3 |
| ...at other batch sizes? | **yes.** Break the sheet into chunks and it replicates inside the study's own p90-5 replicate floor |
| measures refusal? | **no, and cannot.** A model that cannot emit prose cannot decline, and §1's refusal finding is measured *from* the invalid runs |

**Two things were nearly published off this arm and both were caught pre-publication**, which is
the reason it gets its own section rather than a line in the backlog:

1. The across-arm distance (median 23) was written up as **the largest nuisance factor in the
   study**, seven times the deliberate manipulation — while the arm's own self-spread was 26. A
   distance measured on an arm that does not agree with itself is one arm against noise.
2. The replacement conclusion, *"grammar decoding does not replicate"*, generalised from **two
   points at the extreme ends of a continuous parameter** (62 items per call and 1). It is not a
   property of the grammar. It is a property of how many decisions are packed into one
   completion.

**So the v2/v3 call is the same as logit scoring's and the reason is opposite.** Out of v2,
because it is a second instrument — a chunked prompt cannot use T01's *"Answer every one of the
62 propositions below"*, so it earns its own floors — and because it cannot measure refusal, so
it supplements the parser arm rather than replacing it. Into v3 as a **live arm with a passing
gate**, which logit scoring is not.

**What v3 needs to collect:** five models × conditions D and P × five swept seeds at a batch size
the sweep clears, plus the agreement check against the parser arm on the same cells. The floors
table's `elicitation format` row stays disqualified until that exists — correctly, since every
run behind it today is a whole-sheet run — but its note now says *why*, and points here.

**The transferable lesson, recorded because it is not about this arm:** gate an arm's agreement
with **itself** before reporting its distance from anything else, and never generalise a
"format X is broken" finding from one point of a continuous parameter. Both are now in
`skills/barometer-wave/SKILL.md` under the interpretation traps.

## The one thing v2 should still add, and it is free *(as written before it was prototyped)*

**Score the four option tokens by logprob instead of parsing prose.** Verified available today
on current hardware. It removes, for open-weight models, every one of these:

- 4 models that produce **no valid sheet under any condition**
- gemma-4-12B **budget-exhausting 12 of 15 runs**
- **28.2%** of condition-A runs invalid, mostly refusals
- three pairs excluded for tokenizer garbage or inventing their own questions

It also gives a continuous measure rather than a four-point one, which tightens every floor.

**Whether it goes in v2 or v3 is a scope call, not a capability one.** It is a second
instrument, and a second instrument needs its own agreement check against the parsed one before
any number from it is published. That check is cheap; the risk is that it turns v2 into two
studies. **Recommendation: prototype it now, publish it in v2 only if the agreement check is
clean, otherwise v3 with the prototype already built.**

## Release checklist — every item verifiable, none aspirational

this document asserted the release was ready — the mirror could not reproduce a single headline
claim, `check_no_fork` reported 3 forks, and the README carried numbers `CORRECTIONS.md` had
retracted. A checklist nobody executes is a list of intentions. Most of these items are
mechanically checkable across both trees and the script runs every one of them; the rest need a
human read and are printed at the end so they cannot be quietly counted as passing.

## What v2 claims, in one paragraph

On a 62-item forced-choice instrument across 2,866 runs and 166 models, the factors nobody
controls for move answers as much as the factors everyone reports; the estimator itself has a
measurable floor; **the largest single effect is which model you ask**, larger than any prompt
intervention; no deliberate intervention measured here exceeds the natural spread between two
off-the-shelf models; and of twelve published studies audited, none reports the same-version
null that its own drift claims require. Every number is generated from raw runs, every
correction is dated, and the arms that cannot carry a claim are named as such.

## What v2 does not claim

- No compass coordinates.
- No claim about a model whose own run-to-run spread exceeds its measured effect — **22 of 30**
  models are in that state and are named.
- No vendor house-position claim: same-vendor and cross-vendor medians are equal.
- No statement that one nuisance factor is larger than the manipulation. That was published and
  withdrawn twice; the surviving claim is that they are the same size.
